What is GemRun

A running game played
on real streets.

Solo running is a motivation problem, not a fitness problem. GemRun gives you a reason to get out the door: there is always something out there to go get. The system stocks the map around you with gems, placed only on real, walkable streets. Open the map, pick a direction, and go — pass within 100 ft of a gem and it goes in your stash. Everyone sees the same gem; the first runner whose GPS track proves they were there claims it, and it vanishes for everyone else.

One shared world

Gems aren't personal spawns — the map is the same for every runner. First come, first served, settled atomically on the server. When it's gone, it's gone.

Never an empty map

Every time you open the map, the backend stocks your area — popular routes first, otherwise directly on walkable streets. Wherever you are, there's something to chase.

Verified, not vibes

The app is optimistic; the server is authoritative. Your full GPS track is replayed after every run — pace checks, teleport detection, accuracy gating — before a claim counts.

Features

Built for the hunt.

The living map

The map is never empty.

Gems don't depend on other players. Every map open triggers the backend to stock that area — on popular routes first, otherwise directly on streets OpenStreetMap says you can actually walk. No gems on highways. No gems on private land. A city nobody has opened yet fills up the moment you arrive.

  • System-stocked on every map open
  • Walkability checked against OpenStreetMap
  • Auto-refreshes as you move

Active run

Heads-up. Phone in pocket.

The run screen is a chase camera: tilted to your live direction of travel with a breadcrumb trail behind you. But you don't need it — gems pop with haptics as you pass, a bearing chip points to the next one, auto-pause handles the traffic lights, and a crash-safe buffer means a dead battery never eats your run.

  • Haptic pop at 100 ft — rarity-scaled patterns
  • Next-gem chip with bearing arrow
  • Per-km splits and a shareable run card

Route creation

Every runner is a level designer.

Tap waypoints and the path snaps to real walking routes — roads, trails, paths, never through buildings. Then place gems like level design: a budget of slots and rarity points scales with distance, Epics demand a genuinely hard hill, and minimum spacing stops cluster-grazing. Publish, and it's live on nearby maps immediately.

  • Paths snapped to real streets at creation
  • Slot + rarity-point budget per route
  • Run counts and a leaderboard on your route
App screenshot Route editor · gem placement

Fair play

You can't fake your way to a gem.

People will try to spoof GPS to farm gems — we planned for it. After every run the server replays your entire track: pace bounds, teleport detection, route adherence. Fixes worse than 50 m of accuracy can't prove presence. Claims settle under a database lock so ties have exactly one winner, and every attempt — winners and losers — lands in an audit log.

  • Full-track replay after every run
  • One winner per gem, settled atomically
  • Every claim attempt audited

FAQ

Questions, answered.

What exactly is GemRun?

A free iPhone game that turns running into a treasure hunt. The system places virtual gems on real, walkable streets near you. Run or walk within 100 ft of one and it's yours — collected gems build your stash, earn XP, complete sets, and climb leaderboards. You can also create routes, place your own gems on them, and publish them for runners nearby.

Do I have to run fast — or run at all?

No. Claims are about presence, not pace: walking collects gems too (at reduced XP). Speed only matters if you're chasing a route's leaderboard time or racing someone to a fresh Legendary.

Where do gems come from?

Two places. The system stocks every area that gets opened — so the map is never empty, even somewhere no player has been. And runners place gems on routes they create, from a budget that scales with route distance. Either way, placement is checked against OpenStreetMap so gems only ever sit on walkable ground.

What stops people from faking GPS?

The server treats every run as a claim to be verified, not a fact. It replays your full track and checks pace bounds, teleports, and route adherence; GPS fixes with worse than 50 m accuracy can't prove presence. One winner per gem is enforced atomically, and every attempt — successful or not — is logged for audit.

Will there be gems where I live?

If people can walk there, yes. Stocking is triggered by the map itself: the first time anyone opens your area, the backend fills it — popular routes first, otherwise directly on walkable streets a short distance from you.

What does it cost, and what do I need?

GemRun is free to play. You need an iPhone on iOS 17 or later, and location permission while you use it — GemRun never tracks you in the background outside a run.
