What is RunnerCard

A collection you can only build by moving.

RunnerCard is a fitness game where the rewards are real collectible cards. You don't buy packs — you mint them by covering ground inside the day's zones. Every card you own is proof you walked it.

Cards worth chasing

Gems, golden running shoes, wild creatures, artifacts never catalogued before, true facts. Some are common. A few are one in a thousand.

Minted in real zones

Zones sit on public parks and walkable trails — never private or government land. Walk your kilometre inside one and the card is yours.

Fitness that counts

Distance, pace, streak and cards-per-mile — tracked on every hunt. The fitter you get, the more ground you cover, the more you find.

How you mint a card

Four steps. One good walk.

Open the map

A few large zones glow near you — picked fresh every day, anchored on parks and walkable trails.

Get inside one

On foot, on your route. Distance counts with the map open or mid-run — your progress ring fills as you go.

Walk 1 km inside

GPS-verified, walking pace or faster — driving past a park fills nothing. Cross the kilometre and the mint fires.

The card mints

Gem, gear, creature, artifact or fact — rarity rolled at the published odds, your zone, date and distance stamped on it. Yours, permanently.

Zones

Cards live on walkable ground.

A zone is a big patch of the real map that takes its shape from the public park or trail cluster it's anchored on — odd edges and all, grown outward when the park is small so there's always plenty of ground, plenty of footsteps to add. Never on private property, private land, or government land. A fresh set lands around you every day.

Walk a kilometre inside one and its card mints on the spot. No kilometre, no card — which means every collection is a map of the ground you've actually covered.

FAQ

Questions, answered.

Do I have to run to get cards?
No — walking counts. Cover a kilometre inside a zone at walking pace or faster, with the map open or mid-run, and the card mints. Running just covers the ground quicker.
What's a zone?
A large, oddly-shaped patch of the real map — it takes the outline of the public park or trail cluster it's anchored on, and is picked fresh every day. Zones never sit on private property, private land, or government land — only ground you can honestly walk.
What kinds of cards are there?
Five types. Gems are valuable stones with a trade value. Gear — golden shoes, running boots, storm shells — can be equipped to change your next run. Creatures are animals that live along the routes and show only at certain hours. Artifacts are one-of-a-kind discoveries, credited to their first finder forever. Facts are real, verifiable snippets you collect into sets.
Is it free?
RunnerCard is free to play during the beta. There are no packs to buy — the only way to get a card is to go walk it.
Does it actually track my fitness?
It does. Distance, pace, day streak and cards-per-mile are logged on every run, so it works as a genuine running tracker even on the days you don't finish a kilometre in a zone.
