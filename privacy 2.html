Privacy

Last updated August 2026 · applies to the TestFlight beta

The short version: your running life lives on your iPhone. The server sees only what it needs to keep one shared, fair world. No ads, no data brokers, no selling anything to anyone.

What stays on your phone

Your runs, routes, minted cards, binder, settings, and drafts are stored in an on-device database. If you lose signal mid-run, the run is buffered locally — crash-safe — and settles when you're back online.

Apple Health

RunnerCard reads step counts from Apple Health on the device only, to stamp accurate steps onto the cards you mint and your finished runs. Health data is never uploaded, never shared, and never appears on any leaderboard.

Location

RunnerCard asks for "While Using" location permission and nothing more. Your position is used while the map is open and during runs to measure the distance you cover inside zones. There is no background tracking and no location history kept beyond the runs you save. If you deny the permission, the app stays fully browsable.

What leaves your phone

  • The GPS track of a run, sent so the server can verify your claims (pace, teleport, and accuracy checks). It's used for verification and the resulting verdict.
  • Your public profile bits — username, and your best times where you appear on route leaderboards.

That's the whole list.

This website

Essential cookies keep the site working. Optional analytics run only if you choose "Accept all" in the cookie prompt; your choice is stored on this device (you can change it any time via "Cookie preferences" in the footer). The waitlist form stores the email you submit and nothing else.

Deleting your data

Delete your account from Profile → Settings and your server-side data (claims, leaderboard entries, username) is removed. Anything on-device goes when you delete the app.

Questions

Write to hey@runnercard.app — a human reads it.
